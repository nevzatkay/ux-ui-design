# First informal usability trial — scenario and task

**Study stage:** Week 7; first unmoderated-attempt observation, not a structured comparative study.

**Prototype:** https://www.figma.com/proto/XaM92SjirDruf467HMzwZD?node-id=1-4&starting-point-node-id=1%3A4

**Participant:** One other university student, anonymized as `T01`. Recruitment and actual trial are **not yet completed**.

## Research focus

Can a player discover a supported ruling for an UNO card-combination dispute and reach the confirmation state, without verbal navigation help?

## Scenario (provide before the task)

> You are playing UNO with friends. A card combination creates a disagreement, and the group wants to know which rule applies before continuing. You have a small Rulebook prototype available. The deck is standard UNO with no expansion.

## Task (goal, not steps)

> Find out what the rules say about playing a +4 card after a +2 card, check the supporting information, and finish when you feel ready to explain the decision to the group.

**Do not tell the participant:** which button to press, which screen contains the answer, or the exact sequence S01–S05. The question's concrete context is allowed; the UI path is not revealed.

## Facilitator script

> Please use the prototype independently. I will observe, not guide you. If something is unclear, you can say what you expect or are looking for. You can stop at any time. We will record anonymized notes, not your name. The information shown is a static example, not a verified official ruling.

No recording is assumed; obtain permission before taking notes. No pressure to proceed. Do not claim consent has been granted until it is.

## Observation checklist

- Did the participant identify a starting action without prompting?
- Did they recognize the pre-filled question and selected ruleset?
- Did they open the source before declaring the task finished?
- Did they understand that source checking is the confirmation action?
- Where did they hesitate, backtrack, make a wrong click, or ask for help?
- If the participant voluntarily explores an error/empty state, can they recover?

Record **timestamps or order of actions only if actually observed**. Avoid silently correcting or helping. After the task, ask: “Which screen or label, if any, was confusing?” and “What did you expect to happen when you clicked it?” These are follow-up questions, not hints during the task.
