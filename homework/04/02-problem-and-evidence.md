# User story, problem statement, and evidence boundaries

## Research summary — supported

**E1 — REPORTED USER EVIDENCE (Week 2, P01):** During a group UNO game, players disagreed about whether +4 could follow +2.  
**E2 — REPORTED USER EVIDENCE (Week 2, P01):** The group looked online, read the rules, and decided the proposed play was not allowed.  
**LIMIT:** This was a retrospective written response from **one** participant, not live observation. No lookup duration, emotions, exact website, edition or prevalence was measured. The accuracy of the ruling against a specific edition was not independently tested.

**S1 — SECONDARY AUDIT INPUT (Week 3):** In an external rule page audit, locating an exact combination and assessing edition/source provenance emerged as potential UX risks. The audit was not a participant interview and did not include a verified live keyboard test. Do not present S1 as user evidence.

## Main-task user story

> As a player in a group who encounters a disputed card combination, I want to determine whether the move is allowed under the rules we are using and find the relevant rule passage, so that our group can make the next move without guessing.

## Problem statement — required exact structure

> **The problem of the player in a group card game is that a disputed card combination is not clearly resolved at the table, because of which the group interrupts play to search for and interpret rules.**

This statement deliberately describes the user's obstacle and negative consequence, **not the product or its features**.

## Task boundary and testable scenario

**Start:** A participant is told that a +2 card was played in UNO and someone proposes playing a +4 next.  
**End:** The participant decides whether the move is permitted **for the rule edition provided in the test**, points to an accepted passage, or explicitly recognizes insufficient evidence.  
**Outside the task:** scoring the hand, running the match, adjudicating house rules, and every other UNO rule situation.

## Assumptions — not yet validated

- **A1:** A short situational question is easier than browsing a whole rulebook. P01's question was situational, but interface preference was not measured.
- **A2:** Showing the original rule passage improves confidence and agreement. P01 read rules but did not express a preference for a cited UI.
- **A3:** Game/edition selection prevents wrong-version decisions without becoming an extra burden. The interview did not mention edition mismatches.
- **A4:** Players need an answer quickly or they will abandon lookup. Time pressure and actual search duration were not recorded.
- **A5:** Retrieval and citation generation will be accurate for selected permitted documents. This is a technical hypothesis, not research evidence.

**Data ethics:** The participant is anonymized. Original-language quotation permission was not verified in the available interview record; this Week 4 work uses paraphrase only.
