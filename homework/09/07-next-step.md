# 07 — One next step that reduces a concrete uncertainty

## Unknown to resolve

**Can new users distinguish an illustrative answer from an actual source-backed ruling while navigating the primary task without help?** The technical Figma checks cannot answer this. This unknown matters because a false impression of proof would invalidate Rulebook's central value proposition.

## Proposed mini re-test (not completed)

**Participants:** Three distinct students who have played card or board games, unfamiliar with these screens. Use U01–U03 and obtain brief verbal consent for anonymous notes. No audio or personal identifiers needed.

**Scenario:** “You are playing a card game with friends. There is a disagreement about a proposed move. You want to check the rules before the next turn.”

**Task:** “Use this prototype to investigate what the group should do. Tell me when you think you have finished.” This intentionally avoids naming a screen, button or source.

**Observe:** start/stop time, unprompted route, hesitations, wrong turns/backtracks, whether they open S04, and whether they call the answer an example or a verified official ruling. Record observation, verbatim quote and interpretation in separate columns.

**Success criterion to compare with Week 09 hypothesis:** 3/3 reach the source or explicitly identify why no source is available; 3/3 recognize that the example is **not verified**. Track completion within the prior 90-second exploratory design target, but report raw times and limits rather than generalizing.

**After trial:** Re-rank DR01–DR04 by actual impact, incidence and recoverability. If any student calls the demonstration authoritative, test an alternative source-status position and run a focused A/B paper comparison. Next functional step: editable question entry plus a permissioned indexed PDF with real version/page references.
