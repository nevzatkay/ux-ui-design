# Research plan — Week 2 (one page)

**Design decision:** What information should players enter, and what source evidence should the system show to help decide a disputed move during a game?

**Open, non-leading questions:**
1. Tell me about the last time a rule was unclear while playing. What happened?
2. Which exact cards or game situation were involved?
3. What did you and the other players do next?
4. Where did you look, what did you find, and how did you reach the decision?
5. Was anything left unresolved?

**Method:** One short retrospective text interview about a specific past incident, rather than opinions about the app. No direct observation was carried out.

**Participant:** Anonymous participant P01 who played UNO with a group and experienced a dispute about whether a +4 could be played on a +2. Age, occupation and other unrelated demographics were neither required nor recorded.

**Data handling:** Store only anonymized, task-relevant notes under P01. Mark direct quotes in original Turkish, and separate translations, behavioral reports, interpretations and assumptions. No names, contact information, recording or sensitive data. Confirm research-use consent before final academic submission; the visible record has a consent question but no recorded reply.

**Analysis:** Affinity grouping by disputed action, information search and decision; derive insights as observation → interpretation → design consequence. Make a task-based proto-persona and bounded journey. A single recalled example cannot support claims about average duration, feelings, representative patterns or source correctness.

**Next step:** Observe 3–5 rule lookups in live play; note search steps, timing, source selection, edition and use of citations. Test a small prototype with card-combination questions.
