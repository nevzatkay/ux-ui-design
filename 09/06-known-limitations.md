# 06 — Known limitations and evidence boundaries

## Prototype

- It is a **native Figma click-through**, not a running app. It simulates game/edition selection, question asking, retrieval and evidence review.
- Rule question text is **preset**, so a navigation check is not a test of actual form entry or natural-language interpretation.
- Example passages are **not verified** against a licensed, versioned PDF. No real page citation, expansion cross-reference, backend retrieval or rule correctness measurement exists.
- There is no live loading, network response, persistence, validation semantics, screen-reader flow or working keyboard interaction. The Figma design shows target states, not functioning code.
- The component library contains Light/Dark variables, but a full dark-mode screen and a real dark-mode usability test have not been completed.
- Native Figma `Ready for Dev` status could not be changed by the connected editor, so handoff is labeled in the frame rather than falsely claiming a native readiness marker.

## Research and verification

- **Zero completed Week 08 participant sessions** have been supplied to this project; therefore Week 09 has no real participant quotes, observed usability frequency or retest success rates.
- Week 08 DR01–DR04 are **designer screen-inspection findings**, not interview or test findings. Their severity is provisional.
- 4/4 main links, 4/4 exception links and 12/12 variants were verified mechanically in Figma. This confirms structure, not whether someone can complete the task easily or understands an answer.
- The planned 3-person peer session cannot establish broad population performance; interpret the results as exploratory and state limits.

## What not to claim in submission

Do **not** write that “three students tested the Week 08 prototype,” that a specified percentage completed the task, or that a new design improved usability on human data until consented real observations exist.
