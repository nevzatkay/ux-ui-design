# Week 02 — Research provenance, coding decisions, and limits

## The actual evidence available

**P01:** one anonymous retrospective text account of an UNO +2/+4 dispute. The participant reported that the group searched on the internet, read rules, and decided the +4 could not be placed onto the +2. This documents the group's **reported action and decision**, not the correctness of the ruling or a measured user experience.

The public coursework may use an anonymized **paraphrase**. Research-use consent for verbatim quotations has not been confirmed in the accessible record. Do not publish an identifiable quote or attribute a fictitious quotation to this participant.

## Coding codebook

| Code | Definition | Example supported by P01 | What it does NOT establish |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CONTEXT / card combination | A question concerns two named cards and their order | A +2 followed by a +4 was disputed | That the same problem is common among all players |
| ACTION / search | Players searched outside the game | Group looked on the internet | Which site, search terms or duration |
| ACTION / inspect text | Players read rules before deciding | Rules were read | Whether text was official or edition-appropriate |
| OUTCOME / group ruling | A decision followed the lookup | Group decided not to place +4 | Legal correctness or unanimous agreement |
| UNKNOWN / edition | Version or expansion not described | N/A | No basis to treat the edition as standard |
| UNKNOWN / affect & timing | No duration or feelings reported | N/A | No basis to claim frustration or lost minutes |

## Audit trail: report → interpretation → design consequence

1. **Reported:** the dispute concerned a specific card sequence. **Interpretation:** situational wording may be a useful question format. **Design consequence:** show an example question on S02. **Validation needed:** see if independent users understand the input affordance.
2. **Reported:** the group searched the web and read rules. **Interpretation:** the text helped settle the discussion. **Design consequence:** show a cited passage adjacent to each answer. **Validation needed:** whether people actually open it and recognize source limitations.
3. **Not reported:** edition, expansion, rulebook provenance. **Interpretation:** a wrong-version risk remains. **Design consequence:** always expose selected ruleset. **Validation needed:** observe recognition of incorrect edition state.

## Consent and research boundaries

- No names, exact ages, contacts, recordings, or sensitive attributes are required.
- Future participant IDs must not be backfilled with this Week 02 respondent.
- Separate **verbatim**, **reported behavior**, **researcher inference**, **proposed design**, and **unknown** in all later files.
- If research-use consent is later confirmed, record the permission's scope and date separately; do not retrospectively invent it.
- Three or more future observations could explore recurring patterns, but the current data do not support prevalence claims.

## Follow-up task (not performed)

In a live game, observe an actual disputed move; ask neutrally what each player is looking for, which source they consult and whether the final decision is grounded in the selected edition. Record steps and time **only if witnessed**.
