# Affinity map and evidence-based insights

**Legend:** QUOTE = original participant words; REPORTED BEHAVIOR = participant's description; INTERPRETATION = researcher's inference; UNKNOWN/ASSUMPTION = not established.

## Cluster A — The disputed move
- **QUOTE:** “uno oynarken + kartları kullanma kurallarına baktık grupça +2 üstüne + 4 koyuluyor mu koyulmuyor mu diye”
- **REPORTED BEHAVIOR:** Players questioned whether +4 could follow +2 during UNO.
- **INTERPRETATION:** Players can frame rule questions around specific card combinations.

## Cluster B — Looking for evidence
- **QUOTE:** “internetten baktık ve koyulmucağına karar verdik kuralları okuduktan sonra”
- **REPORTED BEHAVIOR:** The group searched on the internet, read rules, and then decided against the move.
- **INTERPRETATION:** Reading rule text formed part of the decision process.

## Cluster C — Unverified details
- **UNKNOWN:** Search website, exact text, edition, time spent, feelings and whether everyone agreed.
- **ASSUMPTION:** A faster lookup or edition-specific reference may reduce interruption or error. This should be tested in a live session.

## Insight 1 — Ask in the language of the situation
**Observation →** The group disputed putting a +4 on a +2.  
**Interpretation →** A specific sequence is a natural starting point for a question.  
**Design consequence →** Allow direct questions such as “Can I play +4 after +2?” and request missing edition/variant context when required.

## Insight 2 — Put evidence next to the answer
**Observation →** The group looked online, read rules, then decided.  
**Interpretation →** Rules text can contribute to a shared decision.  
**Design consequence →** Show an answer with an accessible cited rules passage instead of an unsupported assertion.

**Limit:** Early hypotheses from one retrospective report, not validated generalizations.
