# Research test plan — Week 08

## Learning goals

1. **Wayfinding and comprehension:** Can a relevant player use the prototype without verbal guidance to find an answer state, inspect the rule passage and distinguish example material from verified rules?
2. **Uncertainty and recovery:** When supporting information is missing or an edition is unsupported, does the player understand the limitation and recover without treating an invented answer as fact?

## Participants and sampling

Recruit **at least three distinct students (U01, U02, U03)** who have played card or board games and are not familiar with this prototype. A mix of experience levels is desirable but cannot be asserted before recruiting. Test individually on a mobile-sized display. Record only anonymous participant IDs, no names/ages/contact details. A participant may withdraw at any time.

## Scenario (read verbatim)

> You are playing a card game with friends. Someone questions whether a proposed move is allowed. The group wants to settle the question before the next turn. You have access to this early prototype of a rules-checking service.

## Task (read verbatim; no solution hint)

> Use this prototype to investigate the disputed move and decide what you would tell your friends. Let me know when you are finished.

This instruction does **not** name the screen, button, source, or expected outcome. The prototype contains a preset UNO rule question; this is a known limitation of the test, not a newly collected participant insight.

**Secondary task if time allows:** “Suppose your selected rules do not cover your question. See how you would proceed.” Do not point the participant to the exceptional state.

## Session protocol

1. Read the consent briefing and record explicit yes/no agreement. Do not retain notes on refusal.
2. Give the device at the prototype starting frame S01. Do not demonstrate navigation.
3. Read the scenario and task once; remain silent unless there is a serious blocking technical problem.
4. Note time, path, pauses, wrong turns, backtracking, recovery and spontaneous quotations verbatim.
5. At the end ask only: “What was unclear?” and “What did you think that message meant?”
6. Separate **observation**, **verbatim quote** and **interpretation**; do not convert the researcher's guess into a quote.

## Proposed success criteria and metrics (NOT measurements)

| Metric | Definition / record |
| --- | --- |
| Completion | Reaches S05 and can describe what was reviewed, without facilitator guidance |
| Source understanding | Can identify that the on-screen passage is **sample, not a verified real citation** |
| Uncertainty recognition | Does not assert a definite ruling from the E02 no-source state |
| Time on task | Seconds from first interaction until participant declares task complete |
| Path / misclicks | Ordered frames selected; count wrong turns or redundant actions |
| Hesitations | Approximate duration and screen at hesitation; record observable behavior |
| Verbal prompting | Count of researcher hints (target: zero) |
| Subjective clarity | Short post-task comment, quoted exactly if consented |

**Planned benchmark:** At least 3/3 attempted sessions and no moderator guidance; do not impose a hard performance pass rate on such a small sample. The 90-second target from earlier project planning can be tracked as a *design target*, not as a measured Week 8 result.

## Risks and limits

This is a click-through of **preset content**, not a functional search engine. Editing a question and verifying real source citations cannot be observed in this version. Scores from three people cannot establish population-wide usability. No inference should be drawn from a participant that has not been tested.
